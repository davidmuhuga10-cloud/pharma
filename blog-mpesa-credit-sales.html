Recording M-Pesa, cash and credit sales in your pharmacy

Published 9 November 2026 · Pharma guides

Most pharmacies take payment in more than one way on the same day — some cash, some M-Pesa, some on credit for a regular customer, some through an insurance scheme. The sale itself is simple; keeping an honest record of how it was paid for is where things usually go wrong.

Give every non-cash payment a reference

An M-Pesa payment without its confirmation code recorded is hard to trace later if a customer disputes it. A credit sale without the customer's name attached is money you'll struggle to collect. A bank transfer without a slip number is awkward to match against your statement. The rule is simple: if it isn't cash in the drawer, write down the reference at the time of the sale, not later from memory.

Pharma lets you split one sale across several payment methods — cash, M-Pesa, credit, insurance or bank — each with its own reference, so a customer who pays part cash and part M-Pesa is recorded exactly as it happened.

Keep customer credit on a short leash

Credit sales are where pharmacies quietly lose money, usually not through any one big bad debt but through many small ones that add up. A few habits help:

  • Set a credit limit per customer rather than relying on memory or trust alone.
  • Record every credit sale against the customer by name, not as a loose note.
  • Review balances regularly, not just when a customer disputes what they owe.

In Pharma, every customer has a running credit balance and an optional limit, so a sale that would push someone over their limit doesn't go unnoticed.

Reconcile your till at the end of the day

However you record sales during the day, the real test is whether the cash in the drawer matches what your records say it should be. Open the drawer with a known float, count it at close, and compare. A consistent small gap usually points to a process problem (change not recorded, a sale not entered) rather than one big mistake — and it's far easier to find on the day it happens than a week later.

In short: record a reference for every non-cash payment at the time of sale, keep customer credit on a limit you actually enforce, and reconcile cash daily rather than weekly.
