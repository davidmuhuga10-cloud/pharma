Running a stock take: counting and fixing differences

Published 9 November 2026 · Pharma guides

A stock take compares what your records say you have against what's actually on the shelf. The gap between the two — if there is one — is usually more informative than the count itself.

When to count

A full stock take doesn't need to happen daily, but it shouldn't be rare either. Many pharmacies do a full count monthly, with smaller spot-checks on fast-moving or high-value items more often. The right frequency depends on how much shrinkage or mis-recording you can tolerate before it becomes a real cost.

Counting without closing for the day

Closing to count is the most accurate method but costs you a day of sales. A practical alternative is to count section by section during quiet hours, or to count after close and before open. Whatever method you use, the count has to happen close enough to when you compare it to your records — a count taken over several days while sales continue will never quite match.

Pharma's stock-take feature lets you record counts as you go and compares them against what the system expects, so you can work through sections at your own pace rather than needing the whole pharmacy counted at once.

What a difference usually means

  • A small, spread-out shortfall across many items often points to recording habits — sales entered late, wrong quantities, or returns not logged — rather than theft.
  • A shortfall concentrated on a few high-value items is worth investigating more directly.
  • A surplus (you have more than records say) usually means a received delivery wasn't recorded, or a sale was recorded against the wrong item.

Either way, the fix isn't just correcting the number — it's working out why the gap appeared, so the next stock take has a smaller one.

In short: count regularly enough that gaps stay small, count close to the comparison date, and treat every difference as a clue about your process, not just a number to adjust.
